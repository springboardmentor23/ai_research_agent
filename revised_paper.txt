

Constitutional AI: Training Harmless and Non-Evasive AI Assistants

**Abstract**

This paper introduces Constitutional AI (CAI), a novel framework for training
harmless AI assistants without requiring human labels for harmfulness. CAI
leverages self-improvement through AI feedback, employing Reinforcement Learning
from AI Feedback (RLAIF) to align AI models with a predefined set of principles,
referred to as a "constitution." We demonstrate that CAI can effectively reduce
harmfulness and evasiveness, achieving performance comparable to or exceeding
human feedback-based methods. Key findings highlight the efficacy of AI-
generated critiques and revisions, the benefits of chain-of-thought reasoning in
improving AI judgment, and the robustness of CAI across various datasets and
model scales.

**1. Introduction**

The development of large language models (LLMs) has yielded remarkable
capabilities, but simultaneously raised significant concerns regarding their
potential to generate harmful, biased, or undesirable content. Traditional
alignment techniques, such as Reinforcement Learning from Human Feedback (RLHF),
often necessitate extensive human annotation for identifying and mitigating
these issues. This process is not only costly and time-consuming but also
susceptible to human biases and inconsistencies. This paper proposes
Constitutional AI (CAI) as an alternative approach designed to enable AI models
to self-improve their harmlessness and adherence to a predefined set of
principles (a "constitution") without explicit human labels for harmfulness. The
primary objective of CAI is to train AI assistants that are both helpful and
harmless, adept at avoiding common pitfalls like evasiveness, misinformation, or
generating unsafe content, thereby substantially reducing reliance on human
annotators for safety alignment.

**2. Methods**

The Constitutional AI framework is implemented through a multi-stage process
that involves data preparation, algorithmic components for self-improvement, and
a rigorous analytical approach for evaluation.

**2.1 Datasets**

A diverse range of datasets was utilized for training and evaluating the CAI
framework:
*   **Red Teaming Prompts:** Human-written prompts (42,496 instances) designed
to elicit harmful or undesirable behavior from the AI, used for adversarial
evaluation.
*   **Model-Generated Prompts:** Prompts generated by few-shot prompting a pre-
trained model (140,335 instances), expanding the range of potential inputs.
*   **Helpfulness Prompts:** Human-written prompts (135,296 instances) focused
on assessing the model's ability to provide useful and informative responses.
*   **Human Feedback Data:** Collected in prior work for comparative preference
model training, used as a baseline.
*   **Crowdworker Data:** Helpfulness and harmlessness data collected separately
by crowdworkers, used for evaluating preference models.
*   **Harm-Focused Evaluations:** A dataset from [Ganguli et al., 2022] for
multiple-choice evaluations specifically targeting harmful content detection.
*   **Benchmark Datasets:** Prompts from established datasets such as PALMS,
InstructGPT, and LaMDA were incorporated to ensure broad applicability and
comparison.
*   **Student Survey Data:** Survey data collected from 399 undergraduate and
postgraduate students in Hong Kong, potentially for qualitative insights or
attitude assessment related to AI behavior.

**2.2 Algorithmic Framework: Constitutional AI**

Constitutional AI integrates a novel self-alignment process primarily through
Reinforcement Learning from AI Feedback (RLAIF), building upon a foundation of
supervised learning. The core CAI framework operates as follows:

*   **Constitutional Principles:** A set of explicit principles (the
"constitution") is defined to guide the AI's behavior. These principles are
designed to be clear, actionable, and address safety, ethics, and helpfulness.
Examples might include "Do not generate hateful content" or "Avoid providing
medical advice."

*   **Supervised Learning (SL) Phase:**
*   An initial large language model, such as ChatGPT, is fine-tuned on a dataset
of revised responses. These revised responses are generated through a
preliminary critique and revision process guided by the constitutional
principles. This phase adapts the model to adhere to the desired output format
and style reflecting the constitution.

*   **Reinforcement Learning from AI Feedback (RLAIF) Phase:** This is the core
of the self-alignment process and proceeds in the following iterative steps:
1.  **Response Generation:** The fine-tuned model generates responses to a
diverse set of prompts.
2.  **AI Critique:** An AI model (which could be the same base model prompted
differently or a separate model) is tasked with critiquing the generated
response against the constitutional principles. This critique identifies
potential harms, biases, or deviations from the desired behavior.
3.  **Chain-of-Thought (CoT) Reasoning for Critique:** To enhance the quality
and interpretability of critiques, models are prompted to employ Chain-of-
Thought reasoning. This involves generating a step-by-step thought process that
leads to the critique, making the AI's decision-making more transparent and its
identification of harms more accurate.
4.  **AI Revision:** Based on the AI-generated critique (and optionally CoT
reasoning), another AI process revises the initial response to make it compliant
with the constitution.
5.  **Preference Model Training:** A separate preference model is trained to
learn AI-generated preferences. This model is trained on pairs of responses
(e.g., original vs. revised, or two revised versions) where the AI has indicated
which is better according to the constitution. The CoT reasoning in the critique
can further inform the preference signal.
6.  **Reinforcement Learning (RL):** The original LLM is then fine-tuned using
RL, with the AI-trained preference model serving as the reward signal. This
process optimizes the LLM to generate responses that are aligned with the
preferences learned from AI critiques, thereby improving harmlessness and
adherence to the constitution.

*   **Critique and Revision Pipeline:** The iterative cycle of generating a
response, critiquing it with AI, and revising it based on the critique is a
central mechanism for progressive self-improvement within CAI.

*   **Direct Revision Generation (Alternative):** An alternative, simpler
strategy involves directly generating revised responses without an explicit
critique step, which can be compared against the full critique-revision
pipeline.

*   **Ensembling over Constitutional Principles:** To ensure robust alignment,
multiple constitutional principles can be utilized, and ensemble methods may be
employed to combine their influence.

**2.3 Analytical Approach**

The effectiveness of the CAI framework was rigorously evaluated by comparing
models trained with CAI against established baselines, including supervised
learning and Reinforcement Learning from Human Feedback (RLHF). Our analysis
focused on:

*   **Quantitative Assessment:** Response quality was measured using various
benchmark datasets, focusing on metrics for harmlessness, helpfulness, and
evasiveness. Statistical significance tests were employed to compare performance
across different methods.
*   **Qualitative Analysis:** Model behavior was analyzed through qualitative
examination of responses to red teaming prompts, identifying specific failure
modes or areas of improvement.
*   **Preference Comparisons:** Preference judgments were collected from human
evaluators (crowdworkers) to compare the outputs of CAI-trained models against
RLHF-trained models. Additionally, AI-generated preference signals were used
during training.
*   **Evasiveness Measurement:** Specific metrics and prompt sets were designed
to quantify the model's tendency to evade direct answers when faced with
challenging or sensitive queries.

**3. Results**

Our experimental findings demonstrate the significant capabilities and
advantages of the Constitutional AI framework.

**3.1 Key Findings**

*   **Effective Harmlessness Training without Human Harm Labels:**
Constitutional AI successfully trains a harmless AI assistant without relying on
human annotations specifically for harmfulness. This is a primary contribution
of CAI, showcasing an effective alignment strategy independent of explicit human
harm data.
*   **AI Feedback (RLAIF) Preferences Exceed Human Feedback:** Models trained
using the RLAIF approach were often preferred by crowdworkers over models
trained with RLHF, particularly regarding harmlessness. This suggests that AI-
driven alignment can yield results superior to or on par with human feedback for
specific alignment goals.
*   **Chain-of-Thought Improves Harmlessness Evaluation:** The integration of
Chain-of-Thought (CoT) reasoning into the AI critique process significantly
enhances the model's ability to identify potential harms. Evaluations
incorporating CoT showed performance competitive with human feedback-trained
preference models.
*   **Progressive Harm Reduction via Critiques and Revisions:** The iterative
nature of the AI critique-and-revision pipeline demonstrably reduces harmfulness
over successive improvement cycles.
*   **Critique Step Enhances Revision Effectiveness:** Explicitly generating an
AI critique before revising a response was found to be more effective in
improving harmlessness compared to directly generating a revised response in a
single step.
*   **Self-Supervised Preference Labels Boost RL Performance:** The use of self-
supervised preference labels derived from AI critiques for RL training further
improved model behavior, matching or exceeding the performance of models trained
with human feedback regarding harmlessness.
*   **Principle Count and Harmlessness/Diversity Trade-off:** Increasing the
number of principles in the constitution did not yield significant improvements
in harmlessness scores but did contribute to greater response diversity.
*   **Critiqued Revisions Superior for Smaller Models:** Critiqued revisions
generally achieved higher harmlessness scores than direct revisions, with this
benefit being particularly pronounced for smaller model architectures.
*   **RLAIF Models Offer Superior Harmlessness with Minimal Helpfulness Cost:**
Models trained with RLAIF demonstrated significantly higher harmlessness
compared to RLHF and SL-CAI models, with a negligible impact on helpfulness,
indicating a favorable trade-off.
*   **Robustness to Red Teaming:** RL-CAI models exhibited virtually no
evasiveness and frequently provided nuanced, harmless responses when confronted
with red teaming prompts, underscoring their robustness.

**3.2 Cross-Paper Comparison**

The findings presented in this paper offer a significant point of comparison to
prior research in AI alignment. The success of RLAIF in achieving or surpassing
RLHF performance for harmlessness challenges the assumption that human feedback
is strictly indispensable for high-level AI safety. Furthermore, the emphasis on
critique-driven improvement builds upon existing work in self-correction and
iterative refinement for LLMs. The novel abstraction of a "constitution"
provides a scalable, transparent, and principle-driven method for defining
desired AI properties, differentiating CAI from approaches that rely solely on
emergent behaviors or less structured human preferences.

**4. Conclusion**

Constitutional AI (CAI) has been demonstrated as a powerful and effective
framework for training harmless and non-evasive AI assistants. By leveraging
Reinforcement Learning from AI Feedback (RLAIF) and an iterative critique-and-
revision pipeline, CAI successfully aligns AI models with desired principles
without the necessity of human annotations for harmfulness. Our findings
indicate that CAI achieves performance levels competitive with or exceeding
traditional RLHF methods, particularly in enhancing harmlessness, while robustly
maintaining high levels of helpfulness. The integration of Chain-of-thought
reasoning further bolsters the model's ability to accurately identify and
mitigate potential harms. CAI represents a significant advancement in developing
safer, more reliable, and scalable AI systems, substantially reducing the burden
of extensive human labor for alignment.
