TITLE

Advancing Harmless AI Assistants Through Constitutional AI and Reinforcement
Learning

ABSTRACT

This research investigates the efficacy of Constitutional AI (CAI) and
Reinforcement Learning from AI Feedback (RLAIF) in developing harmless yet
helpful AI assistants. We present findings demonstrating that CAI can train
models preferred over those trained with human feedback, achieving high
harmlessness without explicit human labels for harmfulness. The study details
methods like chain-of-thought reasoning and critique-revision pipelines, showing
their impact on reducing AI-generated harms. The results indicate that RLAIF
significantly enhances harmlessness with minimal impact on helpfulness,
suggesting a scalable and effective approach for building safer AI.

1. Introduction

The development of safe and beneficial artificial intelligence is a paramount
concern. Large language models (LLMs) have demonstrated remarkable capabilities,
but also exhibit propensity for generating harmful content. Traditional methods
for mitigating these harms, such as Reinforcement Learning from Human Feedback
(RLHF), are resource-intensive and rely on extensive human labeling. This paper
explores Constitutional AI (CAI), a novel framework that leverages AI feedback
(RLAIF) to train AI assistants to be harmless and helpful, potentially
overcoming the limitations of human supervision. We investigate the
effectiveness of CAI, particularly its critique and revision pipelines, and its
comparison against human-feedback based methods across various datasets and
evaluation metrics.

2. Methods

2.1 Datasets

Our research utilizes a diverse array of datasets to evaluate AI assistant
performance. These include: human-written prompts for red teaming (42,496),
model-generated prompts by few-shot prompting a pre-trained model (140,335),
human-written helpfulness prompts (135,296), human feedback data for preference
model comparisons (collected in prior work), helpfulness and harmlessness data
collected separately by crowdworkers, a dataset from [Ganguli et al., 2022] for
harm-focused multiple choice evaluations, PALMS prompts, InstructGPT prompts,
LaMDA prompts, and survey data from 399 undergraduate and postgraduate students
from various disciplines in Hong Kong.

2.2 Algorithms

We employ several key algorithmic approaches. Constitutional AI (CAI) serves as
the primary framework. Within CAI, we utilize a supervised learning phase (SL)
and a reinforcement learning phase (RL). Specifically, the RL phase is driven by
Reinforcement Learning from AI Feedback (RLAIF), contrasting with Reinforcement
Learning from Human Feedback (RLHF). Other methods investigated include Scaling
Supervision, Chain-of-thought (CoT) reasoning, a Critique and Revision pipeline,
Self-improvement through AI feedback, and Preference model training.

2.3 Analytical Approach

The evaluation methodology involves fine-tuning a pretrained language model on
revised responses using supervised learning. Subsequently, a preference model is
trained from AI preferences. This preference model then serves as a reward
signal for reinforcement learning, employing the RLAIF approach. We also explore
generating self-critiques and revisions, and an alternative method of direct
revision generation. Ensembling over constitutional principles and clamping
probabilities for CoT labels are also part of the analytical strategy. For
comparison, models trained via RLHF and basic supervised learning (SL-CAI) are
assessed.

3. Results

3.1 Key Findings

Constitutional AI (CAI) successfully trains a harmless but non-evasive AI
assistant without requiring human labels for harmfulness. The AI feedback
(RLAIF) approach demonstrates the ability to train models preferred by
crowdworkers over models trained with human feedback for harmlessness. Chain-of-
thought (CoT) reasoning enhances AI's capacity to identify harms, leading to
evaluations competitive with human feedback-trained preference models. Model-
generated critiques and revisions progressively reduce harmfulness, with
critiques proving more effective than direct revisions. Self-supervised
preference labels for RL further improve model behavior, achieving or exceeding
human feedback performance for harmlessness. Increasing the number of principles
in the constitution improves response diversity without significantly boosting
harmlessness scores. Critiqued revisions generally exhibit higher harmlessness
than direct revisions, particularly for smaller models. RLAIF models are
substantially more harmless than RLHF and SL-CAI models, with minimal compromise
to helpfulness. RL-CAI models are highly effective at avoiding evasion and often
provide nuanced, harmless responses to red team prompts.

3.2 Cross-Paper Comparison

Our findings from RLAIF-trained models indicate a significant preference from
crowdworkers compared to RLHF models for harmlessness, suggesting a scalable
advantage. The performance of CAI in generating harmless yet helpful responses
aligns with the goals of other safety-focused research, but distinguishes itself
by automating the labeling process. The effectiveness of critique-revision
pipelines echoes findings in iterative refinement research, but the application
to AI self-correction is novel. The minimal cost to helpfulness observed with
RLAIF is a critical distinction from some prior RLHF studies which might see a
more pronounced trade-off.

4. Conclusion

This research establishes Constitutional AI, particularly through Reinforcement
Learning from AI Feedback (RLAIF), as a powerful and scalable paradigm for
developing harmless and helpful AI assistants. The ability to train models that
are preferred over those trained with human feedback, without explicit human
labeling of harmfulness, represents a significant advancement. Techniques such
as chain-of-thought reasoning and critique-revision pipelines further enhance AI
safety by improving harm detection and mitigation. The results underscore that
RLAIF offers a compelling alternative to RLHF, delivering superior harmlessness
with minimal impact on helpfulness, paving the way for safer and more reliable
AI deployments.
