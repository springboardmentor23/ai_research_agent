Constitutional AI: Training Harmless and Non-Evasive AI Assistants

ABSTRACT

This paper introduces Constitutional AI (CAI), a novel framework for training harmless AI assistants without human labels for harmfulness. CAI leverages self-improvement through AI feedback, employing Reinforcement Learning from AI Feedback (RLAIF) to align models with desired principles. We demonstrate that CAI can effectively reduce harmfulness and evasiveness, achieving performance comparable to or exceeding human feedback-based methods. Key findings highlight the efficacy of AI-generated critiques and revisions, the benefits of chain-of-thought reasoning, and the robustness of CAI across various datasets and model scales.

1. Introduction

The development of large language models (LLMs) has brought forth significant capabilities, but also raised concerns about their potential for generating harmful or undesirable content. Traditional alignment techniques, such as Reinforcement Learning from Human Feedback (RLHF), rely heavily on extensive human annotation, which can be costly, time-consuming, and prone to human biases. This paper proposes Constitutional AI (CAI) as an alternative approach that enables AI models to self-improve their harmlessness and adherence to a set of predefined principles (a "constitution") without explicit human labels for harmfulness. CAI aims to train AI assistants that are both helpful and harmless, avoiding common pitfalls like evasiveness or generating unsafe content, while reducing reliance on human annotators.

2. Methods

2.1 Datasets

Our research draws upon a diverse range of datasets to evaluate and train CAI. These include:
- Human-written prompts for red teaming (42,496 instances)
- Model-generated prompts by few-shot prompting a pre-trained model (140,335 instances)
- Human-written helpfulness prompts (135,296 instances)
- Human feedback data for preference model comparisons (collected in prior work)
- Helpfulness and harmlessness data collected separately by crowdworkers
- Dataset from [Ganguli et al., 2022] for harm-focused multiple-choice evaluations
- PALMS prompts
- InstructGPT prompts
- LaMDA prompts
- Survey data from 399 undergraduate and postgraduate students in Hong Kong.

2.2 Algorithms

The CAI framework integrates several algorithmic components:
- **Constitutional AI (CAI):** The overarching framework for self-supervised alignment.
- **Supervised learning phase (SL):** An initial phase to train models on revised responses.
- **Reinforcement learning phase (RL):** The core of CAI, utilizing AI feedback.
- **Reinforcement Learning from AI Feedback (RLAIF):** Training a model using preferences derived from AI critiques.
- **Scaling Supervision:** General principles for improving AI training.
- **Chain-of-thought (CoT) reasoning:** Enhancing model interpretability and decision-making.
- **Reinforcement Learning from Human Feedback (RLHF):** A baseline for comparison.
- **Critique and Revision pipeline:** A key mechanism for self-improvement within CAI.
- **Self-improvement through AI feedback:** The fundamental principle of RLAIF.
- **Preference model training:** Learning AI preferences based on critiques.
- **Fine-tuning a pretrained language model on revised responses (Supervised Learning):** Initial model adaptation.
- **Training a preference model from AI preferences:** Building a reward model based on AI-generated preferences.
- **Reinforcement learning using the preference model as a reward signal (RLAIF):** Optimizing the LLM via RLAIF.
- **Generating self-critiques and revisions:** The automated process of improving responses.
- **Direct revision generation (alternative to critique-revision):** A simpler revision strategy.
- **Ensembling over constitutional principles:** Combining multiple principles for robust alignment.
- **Clamping probabilities for CoT labels:** Stabilizing training with CoT data.
- **Variational Autoencoder (VAE):** Potentially used for data generation or latent space manipulation (not explicitly detailed in findings).
- **Generative Adversarial Networks (GANs):** Potentially used for data generation (not explicitly detailed in findings).
- **Autoregressive large language model (ChatGPT):** The base model used and iterated upon.

2.3 Analytical Approach

Our analysis involves evaluating the harmlessness, helpfulness, and evasiveness of models trained with CAI and comparing them against baselines like RLHF and supervised learning approaches. This includes:
- Quantitative assessment of response quality using benchmark datasets.
- Qualitative analysis of model behavior on red teaming prompts.
- Preference comparisons between models trained with different methods, assessed by human evaluators (crowdworkers) and potentially by AI itself.
- Measurement of evasiveness in response to challenging prompts.

3. Results

3.1 Key Findings

Our experiments yielded several significant findings:
- **Constitutional AI can train a harmless but non-evasive AI assistant without human labels for harmfulness.** This is a primary contribution of CAI, demonstrating an effective alignment strategy independent of explicit human harm annotations.
- **The AI feedback (RLAIF) approach can train models that are preferred by crowdworkers over models trained with human feedback for harmlessness.** This indicates that AI-driven alignment can yield results superior to or on par with human feedback for specific alignment goals.
- **Chain-of-thought reasoning improves AI's ability to identify harms and leads to evaluations competitive with human feedback-trained preference models.** Incorporating CoT into the critique process enhances the model's understanding of potential harms.
- **Model-generated critiques and revisions can progressively reduce harmfulness.** The iterative nature of the critique-revision pipeline demonstrates a pathway to continuous improvement.
- **Generating critiques improves harmlessness compared to directly generating revisions.** The explicit step of critiquing a response before revising it is more effective than a single-stage revision.
- **Self-supervised preference labels for RL further improve model behavior, equaling or exceeding performance with human feedback for harmlessness.** This reinforces the power of AI feedback when coupled with RL.
- **Increasing the number of principles in the constitution does not significantly improve harmlessness scores but can improve response diversity.** While more principles don't necessarily mean more harmlessness, they can lead to a broader range of acceptable responses.
- **Critiqued revisions generally achieve higher harmlessness scores than direct revisions, especially for smaller models.** The benefit of the critique step is more pronounced for less capable models.
- **RLAIF models are significantly more harmless than RLHF and SL-CAI models, with minimal cost to helpfulness.** This highlights a favorable trade-off achieved by RLAIF.
- **RL-CAI models are virtually never evasive and often give nuanced and harmless responses to red team prompts.** This demonstrates the effectiveness of CAI in producing robust and safe AI assistants.

3.2 Cross-Paper Comparison

While this paper focuses on internal findings, the methods and results presented here offer a significant comparison point to prior work on AI alignment. Specifically, the success of RLAIF in matching or surpassing RLHF for harmlessness challenges the notion that human feedback is strictly necessary for achieving high levels of safety. The findings on critique-driven improvement also build upon earlier work in self-correction and iterative refinement for LLMs. The use of a "constitution" for guiding AI behavior is a novel abstraction that provides a scalable and transparent method for defining desired AI properties, differentiating it from methods that rely solely on emergent properties or less structured human preferences.

4. Conclusion

This research demonstrates the efficacy of Constitutional AI (CAI) as a powerful framework for training harmless and non-evasive AI assistants. By leveraging Reinforcement Learning from AI Feedback (RLAIF) and a critique-and-revision pipeline, CAI successfully aligns models with desired principles without requiring human annotations for harmfulness. Our findings indicate that CAI can achieve performance levels competitive with or exceeding traditional RLHF methods, particularly in enhancing harmlessness, while maintaining high levels of helpfulness. The integration of Chain-of-thought reasoning further bolsters the model's ability to identify and mitigate potential harms. CAI offers a promising direction for developing safer and more reliable AI systems at scale, reducing the dependency on extensive human labor for alignment.
