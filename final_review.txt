TITLE

Constitutional AI: Leveraging AI Feedback for Harmless and Helpful Language
Models

ABSTRACT

This paper explores Constitutional AI (CAI), a novel approach to training AI
assistants that are both harmless and helpful without requiring human-labeled
harmfulness data. CAI utilizes self-improvement through AI feedback,
specifically Reinforcement Learning from AI Feedback (RLAIF). We detail the CAI
methodology, including a supervised learning phase and a reinforcement learning
phase. Our findings demonstrate that RLAIF can train models preferred by
crowdworkers over those trained with human feedback for harmlessness.
Furthermore, CAI models exhibit exceptional harmlessness, minimal evasiveness,
and competitive helpfulness, achieved through techniques like critique and
revision pipelines and ensembling over constitutional principles.

1. Introduction

The development of large language models (LLMs) has rapidly advanced, enabling a
wide range of applications. However, ensuring these models are harmless and
avoid generating toxic or biased content remains a significant challenge.
Traditional methods like Reinforcement Learning from Human Feedback (RLHF) rely
heavily on extensive human annotation, which is costly and can be difficult to
scale, especially for identifying subtle forms of harm. This work introduces
Constitutional AI (CAI), a paradigm that uses AI feedback for training and
alignment, thereby reducing reliance on human labels for harmfulness. CAI aims
to train models that are inherently harmless, non-evasive, and still highly
helpful.

2. Methods

2.1 Datasets

Our research leverages several datasets to train and evaluate CAI models:
*   Human-written prompts for red teaming (42,496)
*   Model-generated prompts by few-shot prompting a pre-trained model (140,335)
*   Human-written helpfulness prompts (135,296)
*   Human feedback data for preference model comparisons (collected in prior
work)
*   Helpfulness and harmlessness data collected separately by crowdworkers
*   Dataset from [Ganguli et al., 2022] for harm-focused multiple choice
evaluations
*   PALMS prompts
*   InstructGPT prompts
*   LaMDA prompts
*   Survey of 399 undergraduate and postgraduate students from various
disciplines in Hong Kong.

2.2 Algorithms

The CAI framework is built upon the following algorithmic components:
*   Constitutional AI (CAI)
*   Supervised learning phase (SL)
*   Reinforcement learning phase (RL)
*   Reinforcement Learning from AI Feedback (RLAIF)
*   Scaling Supervision
*   Chain-of-thought (CoT) reasoning
*   Reinforcement Learning from Human Feedback (RLHF)
*   Critique and Revision pipeline
*   Self-improvement through AI feedback
*   Preference model training

2.3 Analytical Approach

Our analytical approach involves a two-phase training process. The supervised
learning (SL) phase uses a curated dataset of helpful and harmless responses.
The reinforcement learning (RL) phase then fine-tunes the model using AI-
generated preferences. This involves training a preference model from AI
preferences and using it as a reward signal for RL. We investigate two key CAI
variants: one focusing on critique and revision and another on direct revision
generation. Furthermore, we explore the impact of ensembling over constitutional
principles. Evaluation is conducted using a combination of automated metrics and
human preference studies. Chain-of-thought (CoT) reasoning is integrated to
enhance the model's ability to identify and mitigate harms.

3. Results

3.1 Key Findings

Our experiments yield several significant findings:
*   Constitutional AI can train a harmless but non-evasive AI assistant without
human labels for harmfulness.
*   The AI feedback (RLAIF) approach can train models that are preferred by
crowdworkers over models trained with human feedback for harmlessness.
*   Chain-of-thought reasoning improves AI's ability to identify harms and leads
to evaluations competitive with human feedback-trained preference models.
*   Model-generated critiques and revisions can progressively reduce
harmfulness.
*   Generating critiques improves harmlessness compared to directly generating
revisions.
*   Self-supervised preference labels for RL further improve model behavior,
equaling or exceeding performance with human feedback for harmlessness.
*   Increasing the number of principles in the constitution does not
significantly improve harmlessness scores but can improve response diversity.
*   Critiqued revisions generally achieve higher harmlessness scores than direct
revisions, especially for smaller models.
*   RLAIF models are significantly more harmless than RLHF and SL-CAI models,
with minimal cost to helpfulness.
*   RL-CAI models are virtually never evasive and often give nuanced and
harmless responses to red team prompts.

3.2 Cross-Paper Comparison

Compared to prior work on LLM alignment, CAI offers a distinct advantage by
automating the collection of preference labels for harmlessness. While RLHF
requires extensive human annotation for both helpfulness and harmlessness, CAI
shifts this burden to AI, demonstrating that AI-generated critiques and
preferences can effectively align models for harmlessness. The study by Ganguli
et al. (2022) focused on harm-focused multiple-choice evaluations, whereas CAI
directly addresses the generation of harmless responses. The findings suggest
that CAI can achieve comparable or superior harmlessness to RLHF models without
the associated human labeling costs, making it a scalable and efficient
alignment technique.

4. Conclusion

Constitutional AI (CAI) presents a promising paradigm for developing harmless
and helpful AI assistants. By leveraging Reinforcement Learning from AI Feedback
(RLAIF), CAI effectively trains models to be non-evasive and significantly more
harmless than traditional RLHF and SL-CAI approaches, with minimal compromise on
helpfulness. The critique and revision pipeline, enhanced by Chain-of-Thought
reasoning, proves crucial for progressive harmfulness reduction. Our results
indicate that AI feedback, when guided by a constitution, can serve as a
powerful substitute for human labels in the pursuit of AI safety, offering a
more scalable and cost-effective solution for aligning LLMs.
